# Tips for describing your design

## Quick answer

Be specific about **subject + vibe + reason** — "a sleepy black cat for a chill weekend tee" beats "a cat shirt." Name the mood (cozy, sharp, retro) instead of guessing color codes. Skip technical jargon — the Crew translates intent, not parameters. Stuck? Just start with something rough and iterate, or ask Vibbi for help.

## Details

A loose recipe that works:

- **Lead with the subject.** What's the design *of*? "A meme cat," "my dad's signature," "the vibe of late August in Tokyo."
- **Add the mood.** "Soft and warm," "bold and graphic," "minimalist," "messy and lived-in." Mood guides composition way more than specific colors.
- **Note the use case if it matters.** "For a gift," "for a streetwear hoodie," "for my band's merch." Context shapes the design choices.
- **Skip what you don't need to specify.** The Crew picks placement, scale, manufacturing technique. You only need to call out preferences when you have one.

Bad / better examples:

| Bad | Better |
|---|---|
| "A cool tshirt" | "A neon-noir tiger for a streetwear tee" |
| "Make it red" | "Warm, almost rust — like 70s wallpaper" |
| "Logo for my band" | "Doom-metal band logo, hand-drawn texture, looks like it was etched onto a wall" |
| "Cat" | "A grumpy orange tabby in profile, vintage poster vibes" |

If you're getting stuck before you even type, run a consultation — see [Help me come up with a design](consultation-intro.md). Two or three turns with Vibbi gets you from a vibe to a specific brief.

If the first design isn't right, just describe what's off — "less abstract," "softer palette," "bigger" — and the Crew iterates. No cost to retry.

## When to defer

- "Why didn't this work?" → ask the Crew to iterate; describe what's off.
- "Show me successful prompts other people used" → not customer-facing data; redirect to public gallery (if available) or the consultation path.
- Specific technical questions about how prompts are parsed → not customer-facing.
